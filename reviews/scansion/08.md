# Book VIII: scansion review and delivery tags

I checked all 1085 verses: every `var`, `amb` and `frag` row, and every `ok` row against the Latin. **84 verses were corrected** in `scan/manual/08.tsv`. `python3 tools/check_manual.py 08` reports `08 OK`.

## Corrections by group

**1. Elision before consonantal *i* (12 verses).** The scanner elided a vowel before *iacens, iocose, Iacintus, iactat, iacet, iacta, Iovem, iacuisse, iocari*. It treated the *i* as a vowel. Each verse was restored to an unelided reading with the same metre, such as `pas|símque ia|céns`, `Ílle io|cóse`, `ménia |iáctat` and `dére Io|vís`.
Verses: 17, 176, 333, 378, 394, 453, 474, 527, 537, 766, 834, 887.

**2. Wrong u/v decisions (8 verses).**
- 88: *belua*, not *belva*. This gives a dactyl in the 5th foot.
- 187: *ambigue*, not *ambigve*.
- 203 and 706: *precipue*, not *precipve*.
- 470: *diluvi'*, not *dilvui'*.
- 536: *dissiluit*, not *dissilvit*.
- 956: *siluere*, the perfect of *sileo*, not *silvere*.
- 730: *iuit* is *ivit* ("he went", as in the translation). This verse also needed a full rescansion.

**3. Medial consonantal *i* read as a vowel (4 verses).** These are *iniuria* (341, 622), *periuris* (843) and *abicitis* (657). The correct forms are `in|iúria`, `Périu|rís` and `l'ábici|tís`.

**4. Wrong scansion: a false quantity under the ictus, or the wrong `amb` candidate (31 verses).**

- 89: *Gaetūli*. Now SDSSDX.
- 125: *clĭpeum*.
- 162: *Arctoas* is three syllables, not a synizesis, and the ictus was on short *ge* in *vergere*. Now SSSSDX.
- 168: *rēgĭbus Ēōis*.
- 223: *Boetem* has three syllables. The scanner had turned it into *Betem*.
- 277: *ūno* and *sĭquidem*.

**5. Caesura dash only (29 verses).**
- 6 verses: the dash was removed because punctuation already stands at the penthemimeral. The scanner missed punctuation after a prodelided `'st`, a quote mark, a ` - ` or a semicolon. Verses: 56, 66, 109, 432, 673, 724.
- 23 verses: the dash stood right after a word that cannot carry a caesura (*et, aut, ac, ex, in, de, si, qua, postquam, donec, quoniam, nondum*). Where a hephthemimeral exists, the dash was moved there. Otherwise it was removed.
  - Moved to the hephthemimeral: 122, 318, 321, 456, 517, 540, 747, 922, 937, 1004, 1011, 1060, 1083.
  - Removed: 108, 170, 198, 568, 614, 692, 731, 739, 868, 1074.
- Further dash changes went with the rescansions in 203, 277, 391, 527, 698, 730 and 956.

## Uncertain or left unchanged
- 31 *aratrum*: the verse needs *ără-* although the second *a* is long. This is Petrarch's quantity, so the line is left as scanned.
- 110 *labor est* at verse end: a short *bo* begins the 6th foot. Left.
- 138 *Convivia*: the long *vī* stands in the short position. A synizesis reading would give the same ictus places, so the line is left.
- 153, 160, 359 *Ăsia* with a short A: this is Petrarch's consistent usage. Left.
- 168 / 358 *Eois*: line 168 needs a long *Ē*, while line 358 has a short *Ĕ* through the elision. Both quantities are attested.
- 339 *Mathona*, 943 *Clusio* (short *Clu*), 772 *generis* (final syllable lengthened under the ictus), 694 *introiere* (consonantal *i*): each is the only possible scansion. Left.
- 911 *Praxitelis opus Phidieque*: no regular scansion exists. I kept the scanner's DSDSDX, which lengthens *lis* and *o*.
- 624: the hiatus reading at the caesura is my choice. Without it the ictus falls on short *e* of *erat*.
- I followed the rule order for the dash: penthemimeral first. As a result, a few dashes separate an adjective from its noun, as in 162, 472, 813 and 929. This is accepted verse practice, not an error.
- Proof-reading bars in rows I did not otherwise change, such as `ping|uí` (476) and `coh|órtes` (13), were left as they are. They do not affect the final text.

## Tags (`tags/08.tsv`)
There are 46 section tags, one at the first line of each section. There are 27 inner tags, about one per 40 verses, and no line has two tags.
- **Narrative sections:** `stately`, `calm narration`, `measured`, `vigorous`, `reverent`, `majestic`. The Rome tour (863–952) alternates between `reverent` and `stately`.
- **Speeches:**
  - Laelius: `warm, admiring` (47) and `proud` (221).
  - Scipio's judgement of Hannibal: `earnest`. Of Alexander: `indignant`, then `scornful`.
  - Hannibal before the senate: `weary, resigned` (277).
  - Scipio to the envoys: `stern`, then `measured` at "Nos equidem… parcimus".
  - Hasdrubal: `pleading` (701, 807), `bitter, scornful` (762), `grateful` (847), and `wistful, reverent` for the farewell to Rome (993).
  - Scipio's parting words: `earnest`, then `tender` at the handshake (1066).
- **Mood shifts:**
  - `ironic`: Africa "vomits back" its plunder (28), and the ambition of Claudius and Lentulus (483, 557).
  - `bitter`: "Heu pestis…" (568).
  - `agitated` for the storm (501), then `fearful` (521).
  - `sorrowful` / `grieving`: the death of Vermina, the weeping envoys, the captives, and the burning fleet (1082).
  - `awed`: entering Rome (858), and the descent into hell (1000).
