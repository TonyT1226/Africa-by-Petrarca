# Book VII: scansion review and delivery tags

I read all 1131 verses. Two mechanical passes backed up the reading: one listed every ictus syllable that is not long by position, and one listed every closed syllable that sits in a dactyl's short slot. I corrected **85 verses** in `scan/manual/07.tsv`. `check_manual.py 07` prints `07 OK`.

**4. Caesura dash only (33).**
- The dash stood right after a preposition, a conjunction or *et*, or after *tot*, *quem* or *non*. I moved it to the hephthemimeral or removed it: 5, 220, 227, 228, 262, 289, 342, 356, 357, 387, 429, 465, 507, 516, 609, 613, 614, 683, 706, 740, 779, 833, 868, 876, 909, 969.

- 423 and 925: a dash split a noun phrase right next to a strong full stop, so I removed it.

## Uncertain or left as they were
- **288** (irr): the line cannot be scanned as printed. With `res firmet utrimque` it scans cleanly as DDDSDX, so `firment` is probably a misprint. That emendation belongs in the text pipeline, so I left the row plain.
- **500** is a lacuna. **1117** is a half-line and I kept it, with Lĕlius short as in 889.
- Kept as Petrarch's licence, with no better scansion available:
  - 702 `tuum sĕnescere`: the spondee needs sĕ long.
  - 1020 `tubicine`: four short syllables.
  - 1065 `morere`: three short syllables in a row.
  - 386 and 823 `Egătis`.
  - Scĭpio with a short first syllable: 144, 197, 367, 1106.
- Judgement calls I made:
  - 202: I put the dash at the hephthemimeral (`suás — tam`) for sense, not the trochaic.
  - 420: final o shortened in `ignorŏ`.
  - 555: `Prō-` lengthened.
  - 651: synizesis.
- Scanner patterns that may recur in other books:
  - `NO_INIT_J` "io" catches *iocantis*.
  - `SU_CONSONANT` "suas" catches the possessive *suas*.
  - It reads ab- + i as consonantal i (*abiens*).
  - It elides before a consonantal I or i.
  - It ignores word ends at a prodelision when placing the caesura.

## Delivery tags (`tags/07.tsv`)
There are 93 tags: one at each of the 48 section starts and 45 inside sections, which averages one inner tag every 25 verses.
- **Narrative frame:**
  - wistful (homecoming), tense (omen), grave and foreboding (Roman fears, Fabius)
  - reverent (thanksgiving)
  - calm narration (scouts and camps), majestic (the meeting of the two generals), hushed (their silence)
- **Speeches:**
  - Hannibal: stately, rueful, earnest, then pleading, grave, measured
  - Scipio's reply: stern, then fierce (the sea of blood), ironic, proud, scornful and defiant
- **Night and heaven:**
  - reverent (the Muses), majestic (the two matrons)
  - Carthage: indignant, wistful, proud, anxious
  - Rome: pleading, grateful, grieving, reverent, scornful
  - Jupiter: majestic and serene, gently ironic; grave and foreboding for the exiles; reverent and hushed, then tender, for the Incarnation
- **Battle day:**
  - foreboding (dawn), stately and majestic (Scipio's array)
  - confident, then triumphant (Scipio's "we have won")
  - menacing (Hannibal), defiant, commanding, impassioned (his appeals to each nation)
  - tense (the trumpets), fierce (the clash), bitter and sorrowful (the poet's lament)
  - grim (the river of blood)
  - urgent, then steady (Scipio's recall)
  - solemn (the fate of the world), majestic (the duel)
  - proud and disdainful (against Greece)
  - angry and fierce (the rout)
  - sorrowful and weary (Hannibal's flight)
