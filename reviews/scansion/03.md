# Book III: scansion review and delivery tags

I checked all 802 verses against the Latin, with special attention to the 17 `amb`, 15 `var`, 3 `frag` and 1 `irr` rows. `scan/manual/03.tsv` overrides **70 verses**. `python3 tools/check_manual.py 03` reports `03 OK`.

## Corrections, grouped by cause

**1. Word-initial vocalic V read as a consonant (27 verses).** This is a scanner bug. An uppercase V before a consonant becomes `v`, so words like *Vbi, Vlla, Vndique, Vmbra, Vt, Vrbs, Vltor, Vnus, Vlixes* lose a syllable. That breaks the foot count and puts accents in the wrong places (`Vllá`, `Vt fáci…`). Fixed: 63, 69, 138, 142, 171, 232, 253, 321, 324, 351, 363, 376, 387, 399, 414, 422, 437, 516, 539, 549, 551, 571, 576, 584, 625, 755, 800.
- Several of these also had knock-on errors that the fix removes:
  - 142 *Ydeum* forced as `I-de-um`.
  - 363 *diei* "relaxed".
  - 376: 5th foot spondaic and *fit* treated as long.
  - 549 *conterruit* variant and an impossible `fóro`.
  - 625 *nequeas* synizesis.
  - 755: spondaic line with *aliis* forced.
- All of these now scan naturally.

**2. Other wrong vowel or consonant decisions (13 verses, plus 324 from group 1).**
- 200, 210, 580: *Iovis* has a consonantal I, so there is no elision before it. The text now reads `Cára Iovís`, `prognáta Iovís`, `templúmque Iovís`.
- 262, 410: *iacet* has a consonantal i. In 410, `Immensoque iacet` has no elision.
- 355, 636: *Iuissem* and *iuisse* are *ivissem* and *ivisse* (`Ívis|sém`, `i|víss'in`).
- 522, 799: *precipue* has a vocalic u (`Précipu…`), not `Precipv`.
- 16: *iniuria* is `in-iú-ri-a` (pattern DSDSDX). The audio text is unchanged.
- 184, 324: in *Marmoreusque* and *clipeusque*, `-eus-` is not a diphthong.
- 373: *Meroe* has three syllables (`Meroé`).
- 325: *deinceps* has three syllables, and *chalybum* has two short vowels.

**4. Caesura dash (10 verses).**
- The scanner does not see a word end after a prodelided `'st`. So it added a second dash even where the penthemimeral caesura already has punctuation. I removed the dash in 20, 163, 245, 293, 496, 699 and 726.
- Dash right after a preposition:
  - 362 `ad — nostras`: moved to the hephthemimeral caesura.
  - 483 `in — populo`: removed, because the comma at the hephthemimeral caesura already marks the break.
- 558: moved from `qua — ratione` to the real penthemimeral caesura after *explendum'st*.

**5. Lacunae.**
- 78 (`< >`): scanned as `Hósti|l'ín patri|ám — trans|gréssus … ea |méns est`. The metre suggests exactly one long syllable is missing before *ea*, which stays unaccented. The old row had random accents.
- 226: `NOSCAN Plaudebant in cantu, multumque Diane`. The line has only 12 syllables and one long is missing, which agrees with the translator's note. The scanner had forced a spondaic line with `qué` under ictus.

## Left unchanged (uncertain or acceptable)
- **87** (two half-lines joined by ` … `) happens to make a correct DSDSDX verse. The `…` stands at the penthemimeral caesura, so I kept it.
- **89 and 630** are correct half-lines.
- **779** (`irr`): *finit* cannot be scanned. With the conjecture *init* (translator's note), the line scans perfectly: `Prímus i|nít fas|cés — auc|tór qui |prímus a|dépte` (DSSSDX). The translator chose not to emend, so I left the plain row alone. Adding that line to the manual file applies the conjecture.
- **Licences I accepted:**
  - 22 and 24: Petrarch scans *Laelius* both ways; *Lĕlius* is common elsewhere.
  - 29: *Militia. Et* has 13 syllables, which forces two licences.
  - 168: *Cirrheoque* and *rēsupinus*.
  - 225: *Horeades*.
  - 371: *cratĕres*.
  - 388: *Gerione*.
  - 600: *Victato* (= *vittato*).
- **Proof-reading bars only:** I did not touch rows whose spoken text is already right but where the `|` is slightly off (`mich|í`, 424 `Ini|úria`, 404 `A|tlás`).
- **Dashes after elided *et* or *ac*** (`capit'ét —`, `cel'ác —`) are a conventional penthemimeral caesura, compare *multum ille et | terris* (*Aeneid* I.3). I kept them.
- **Trochaic dashes** in verses that also have a punctuated trithemimeral caesura are also kept (50, 158, 162, 358, 456).

## Tags (`tags/03.tsv`, 69 lines)
There are 27 section-start tags and 42 inner tags, about one inner tag every 19 verses.
- **Narrative frame:**
  - *stately* for the dawn.
  - *calm narration* for the voyage, the arrival and the end of the song.
  - *awed* and *marveling* for the jewelled ceiling and zodiac.
  - *stately* and *majestic* for the golden gods.
  - *hushed, ominous* for the underworld images.
- **Speeches:**
  - Scipio moves through *wistful*, *earnest*, *fierce* and *confident*.
  - Laelius's embassy is *courtly, persuasive* and then *proud*; Syphax is *courteous, measured*, then *regretful*.
  - The singer's song is *vigorous, heroic* (Hercules), *wistful* and *indignant* (Dido), then *tense* and *foreboding* (the Punic War).
  - Laelius's history of Rome is *solemn*, *proud*, *exultant* (the Scipios) and *reverent*.
- **Episodes:**
  - Curtius: *tense, foreboding*, then *ominous* (the soothsayer), *defiant* (the youth), *awed* (the chasm closing) and *reverent*.
  - The Decii: *solemn, heroic*, then *exultant*.
  - Regulus: *grave*, *grieving*, then *proud, defiant*.
  - Tarquin: *bitter*, *reflective* (the bee simile), then *resolute*.
  - Lucretia: *grave, foreboding*, *anguished*, *tense*, *sorrowful*, *resolute*, then *hushed* at the suicide.
  - Brutus: *stern* and *fierce* (the oath), *indignant*, *triumphant*.
  - The Republic's founding: *stately*, *grave* (the execution of his sons), *fierce* (the duel), *sorrowful* (the mourning).

## Note for the other books (scanner bugs)
- An uppercase V before a consonant is not treated as a vowel.
- `NO_INIT_J` wrongly makes *Iovis/Iove* and *iacet/iacens* vocalic.
- `-eusque` and `-eus` before a consonant are treated as a diphthong.
- After a prodelided `'st` with punctuation, the penthemimeral caesura is not recognised.
- The *precipue* u is treated as consonantal.
