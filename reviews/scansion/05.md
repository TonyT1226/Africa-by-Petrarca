# Book V: scansion check and delivery tags

I read all 773 verses against the Latin and the translation. The 28 `amb` and 7 `var` rows got special attention, and I skimmed every `ok` row for natural quantities. I corrected **60 verses** in `scan/manual/05.tsv`. `python3 tools/check_manual.py 05` prints `05 OK`.

## Corrections (60)

### A. Wrong quantities or wrong foot pattern (17)
The scanner knows only quantities by position. In these lines it put a naturally short vowel under the ictus, or a long vowel in a dactyl's thesis.
- **21** *Phoebēa*: SDDSDX (`Néc Fe|béa fo|rét`). The old version had a spondee *á fo*.
- **35** *praeclārē*: DDSSDX (`quíd refe|rám — pre|cláre`).
- **39** *Inque Medūsaeum*: DSSDDX (`Ínque Me|dúse|úm`). The old version had *-que* under the ictus.
- **110** *rēgīna*: SSDSDX (`"Re|gína, pre|cór`).
- **148** *plācāta*: SSDSDX.
- **226** *captīvae*: SSDSDX (`Cápti|vé si |tántus ho|nós`).
- **249** *prŏperans*, *Atlantēō*: DSDSDX (`Púlcer A|tlánte|ó — prope|ráns`). A is short before *thl*.
- **355** *fāmae*, *sŭperum*: DDSDDX (`sa|crí, fa|mé, supe|rúmque`).
- **434** *ūnīus*, *crīmĭne*: DDSSDX. This also removes a spondaic 5th foot on a short *mi*.
- **468** *Rōmānā*, *fŏre*: DSDDDX (`Róma|ná — fore |cúm`).
- **511** *lacrĭmis*, *sēse*: DDSSDX.

- **652** *Fortūnāte*, *Charōn*, *ŭtinam*: SDDDDX.

### B. Wrong syllable count: consonant or vowel i/u, hiatus, diaeresis (20)
- **Initial consonantal i read as a vowel**, which gave a false elision: 23 and 585 (*Iovi*), 748 (*Iovis*), 56 and 318 (*iacet*), 145 (*iacens*), 635 (*iocundum*). In 56 the pattern changes to SDDSDX. In the others only the elision is undone, for example `mi|ránda Io|ví`.
- ***iniuria* = in-jū-ri-a** (4 syllables, not 5): 90 and 753. Both are now DDSSDX (`for|tún'in|iúria`).
- **Vocalic u**:
  - 325 *voluisti* (from *volo*), not *volvisti*: `volu|íst'?`.
  - 403 and 498 *praecipue*, not *praecipve*.
  - 524 *viduae*, not *vidvae*.
  - 194 and 486 *suas* is two syllables. The scanner glided it like *suadeo*. In 486, *Noctī* is also long.
- **Words that need all their syllables**:
  - 192 *aeno* is *a-ē-no* (the transmitted spelling is *aeno*). Written `a|éno`, the line is DSDSDX. The old version had a spondaic 5th foot on short *si*.
  - 202 *Hymenaeus* has 4 syllables, SSDDDX (`Speci|ós'Hime|néus`).
  - 309 *Aegaeis* has 3 syllables, SDDDDX (`Ége|ís ceci|dísse`).
  - 749 *tropaeis* has 3 syllables, DDDSDX (`gráta tro|féis`).
- **735** *Caelicolae* is not elided. Hiatus at the sense pause gives natural quantities everywhere: SDSDDX (`Célico|lé — et |quí mari'|ámpla`). The old version needed *mă-rĭ* long.

### C. Caesura dash in a bad place (21)
- **Dash right after a preposition or conjunction, moved to the hephthemimeral.**
  - 4 *ad — portas* → `portás — custóde relícto`.
  - 272 *et — Stygiam* → `Stigiám — tetigísse`.
  - 333 and 394 *inter — stragem / fremitum*.
  - 429, 734, 739 and 757 *et — …*.
- **Same problem, but the only other caesura would split a noun phrase, or a comma already marks the pause.** The dash is dropped:
  - after *et*: 170, 292, 330, 491, 513, 519, 762;
  - after a preposition: 94 (*in — finem*), 189 (*inter — curas*), 281 (*inter — patrios*), 361 (*in — Martem*);
  - after a conjunction: 397 (*quod — blande*).
- **432**: the real penthemimeral is after the prodelision *magni'st*, not *pars — non*. The scanner skips word ends after *-'st*.

### D. Other (2)
- **124** is defective: only five feet (see the TEXTNOTES), so it is now `NOSCAN` plain text.
- **271**: display of the prodelision across the semicolon. It was `tró'; st`, now `tró;'st`.

## Uncertain or left as scanned
- **262** *portendĕre aliquid*: three shorts in a row, so it does not scan with a short *e*. The scanner treats it as *portendēre*, which is the only possible reading. Left as scanned.
- **498** *in rege regisque*: this scans only with *regĕ* lengthened at the penthemimeral. Kept, now with vocalic *praecipue*.
- **156** *non dire Caribdis*: the metre and the grammar need nominative *dira*. The scansion (short final) is right as it stands.
- **667** *visa tangere iuventa*: correct as scanned. *tangēre* is the future passive (see the translator's note).
- **Two lines whose text is already right, so they are not in the manual file:**
  - 63 *subterrāneo*: synizesis *-neo*, pattern SSDDDX. The scanner's accents are identical.
  - 675 *Rhodopēĭus*: five syllables, pattern DDSDDX instead of DDSDSX. The text is identical.
- **601** *loco; et — quanquam*: the dash after *et* should go. But any manual row there fails the checker's first-vowel rule on *Suéta*, because the u is a glide, not a consonant to be written v. Left alone.
- **Name quantities vary, as Petrarch has them:**
  - *Sy-* in *Syphax* is long in 295 but short in 325 and 418.
  - *So-pho-* in *Sophonisba* is long in 535 and 584 but short in 629.
  - *Massinissa* has *-si-* long.
- **Note**: `tools/scan.py` was changed while I worked (NO_INIT_J no longer covers *io-* and *iac-*). A rerun of `recite.py` may already produce some of the B-group fixes. The manual rows give the same text.

## Delivery tags (`tags/05.tsv`, 73 tags)
There are 32 section-start tags and 41 inner tags, about one inner tag per 19 verses.
- **Narrative arc:** `stately` (entry into Cirta) → `awed` (the queen's beauty) → `warm` (love at first sight) → `pleading` (her plea) → `tender` (his promise) → `sorrowful` (her wish to die) → `tense` (storm of love) → `urgent` (self-persuasion) → `subdued` (hasty wedding).
- **Rumour and Syphax:** `brisk` (rumour) → `vigorous` (Syphax led in) → `grave` (Scipio questions him) → `bitter` (Syphax blames the woman).
- **Scipio and Massinissa:** `calm narration` → `grave, earnest` (on pleasure) → `firm` (conquer yourself) → `sorrowful` (Massinissa yields) → `grave, foreboding` (the condemned man simile).
- **Night:** `wistful` (Phoebus and Daphne) → `pensive` (Scipio's night) → `anguished` (Massinissa's night).
- **Laments I–VII:** `tender, grieving`; `agitated`; `indignant`; `hushed, foreboding`; `desperate`; `grieving`; `fervent`.
- **Ending:** `hushed` (dawn, the cup) → `tense` (Sophonisba receives it) → `bitter, defiant` (curse on Scipio) → `fierce` (curse on Massinissa).
- **Inner tags** go at the first line of a speech or a turn in mood. Examples: 6 `menacing` (wolf simile), 211 `exultant`, 253 `foreboding`, 325 `stern`, 366 `indignant`, 555 `ironic`, 574 `defiant`, 680 `rueful`, 727 `proud, calm`, 735 `solemn`, 767 `hushed` (her death).
