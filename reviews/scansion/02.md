# Book II: scansion review and delivery tags

All 557 verses were checked against natural quantities. I printed the scanner's syllable quantities next to every line and checked each syllable the scanner had guessed (`?`) against the real vowel length. All `var`/`amb` rows were checked as well.

**39 verses corrected** in `scan/manual/02.tsv`. `check_manual.py 02` reports OK.

## 1. Wrong scansion: the scanner picked a pattern that needs a false quantity (12)
The scanner has no vowel-length lexicon. When several patterns fit the syllable count, it falls back on rules of thumb such as "final -a is short", and these sometimes fail.
- 22 `sub geminā … ruinā`: the -a is ablative (long), so the line is DDSSDX (`súb gemi|ná — nu|távit`). The scanner had put the short *ge* in a long slot.
- 73 `Lītŏră, quā Grāīs`: DSDDDX. The scanner had made *-to-* long and *qua* short.
- 105 `mīrīs`: DSDDDX (`tó mi|rís — ani|múm`).
- 198 `Maeōtida`: DDDSDX (`ét Me|ótida |dúram`). The scanner had made *ō* short and *-da* long.
- 213 `Aegaeī … vĕlŭt`: DSSDDX (`Ége|í, ce|ló velut`). The scanner had made *velut* a spondee and was at penalty 2.
- 287 `Dēpōne`: DSDDDX. The accent moves from *Deponé* to *Depóne*.
- 304 `Cīvīlī ŏdĭō`: SDSDDX (`Cívi|líqu'odi'|ét bel|lís — furi…`).
- 358 `tōtīus` with long *ī*: DSSSDX (`to|tíus |sóla sit`). The scanner's version had a spondee in the 5th foot and short syllables in long slots.
- 384 `occāsūs, ŭbi`: DSDDDX.
- 397 `Extrēmā (… in Arcto)`: DSDDDX (`Éxtre|má — quis e|rít`).
- 470 `locorumque` is hypermetric: *-que* elides into *Estis* at the start of 471. The line is DSSSDX (`ígno|tós? An|nórum, |náte, lo|córumque`), and *-que* rides unaccented in the 6th segment. The scanner's version had long *nāte* and a short *-nō-* in *ignōtōs*.
- 494 `cūrīs`: DSDDDX (`múm cu|rís — one|rát`).

## 2. Wrong syllable division: u/v, i/j and diphthongs (8)
- 108 `Praecipue` was rendered as **Précipvé** (consonantal *u*) and `iocosum` was treated as starting with a vowel, so *illa* was wrongly elided. Corrected to `Précipu|é tam |rára qui|dém. Ver'|ílla io|cósum`.
- 349 `pulvisque` was rendered as **puluísque** (vocalic *u*). Now `pul|vísque`, SSDDDX.
- 461 `Diluvia` was rendered as **Dílvui'**. Now `Díluvi'`, with the same accents.
- 434 The scanner read `suas` as consonantal *sv*, as in *suadeo*. Now `ípsa — su|ás`, DDDSDX.
- 153 `Caesareumque` and 214 `clipeusque`: the scanner had merged *eu* into one syllable. Both now have *ĕ-ŭ* in hiatus: `Césare|úmque` (DDSDDX) and `clipe|úsque` (DSDDDX).
- 199 `Rīphaeōsque`: the *ae* stays one long syllable, as in Virgil's *Rīphaeasque*. The spelling becomes `Rífe|ósque` (*ae* → *e*) instead of the split `Rífae`.
- 194 The scanner's version needed a long *te* in *tĕnax*. The line now has natural *Iūdaea* (`Vínce|túr Iu|déa te|náx, Ar|ménia`), taking *Armēnia* as a licence on a foreign name.

## 3. Caesura dash in a bad place (19)
The scanner puts the dash at the penthemimeral caesura even when the word before it is a proclitic, which makes the voice pause after *et / ac / in / ad / si / ut / inter*.
- **Dash removed** because no good alternative exists, or punctuation already gives the pause: 102, 124, 134, 239, 258, 307, 426, 489, 526 (after *et / ac / ad / si*); 516 (trihemimeral fallback between *alterno … convictu*); 514 (a trochaic dash between *partasque — cole*, followed directly by the semicolon); 362 (the penthemimeral `situ'st,` is already punctuated, but the scanner missed it because of the prodelision).
- **Dash moved to the hephthemimeral caesura:** 66 `Rabiés —`, 201 `regnúm —`, 206 `medios —` (it had been after *inter*), 223 `terrá —`, 272 `gladiís —`, 282 `Stygiás —` (it had been after *in*), 363 `parvús —` (it had been after *ut*).

## Delivery tags (`tags/02.tsv`)
There are 60 tags: one at the start of each of the 25 sections and 35 inside sections, about one per 16 verses. All are 1–3 words.
- **Opening dialogue:** gentle / pleading / anxious / desperate (Scipio, ll. 13–30).
- **The father's prophecy:** resolute, then wary for Hannibal's tricks. Then:
  - majestic and tense for the battle of the Virtues, menacing for the Vices
  - triumphant, with a fierce tag at the snake simile
  - bitter for Fama
- **The procession of Roman history:**
  - stately, proud and admiring for Marius, Pompey and Caesar
  - sorrowful, then indignant, at the civil war
  - majestic and serene for Augustus and the closing of Janus
  - sorrowful and foreboding for the decline, warm and proud for Vespasian and Titus, bitter and indignant for the foreign emperors
- **Scipio's outburst and the father's answer:** grieving, then desperate; consoling and reassuring for the lion simile and Rome's survival.
- **The descent and the discourse on fame:**
  - hushed and awed, then tender
  - grave and meditative, with ironic turns (`Finge quod esse potest`, `ridenda insania`)
  - reflective and sorrowful, then wistful and uplifting (`Vivite sed melius`)
  - foreboding for the floods that destroy books
  - tender and awed for the "second Ennius"
- **Exhortation and ending:**
  - earnest and tender for friendship
  - reverent for Scipio's reply, sorrowful for the ungrateful fatherland, defiant at the exile's revenge
  - brisk for dawn and the trumpet that wakes him
