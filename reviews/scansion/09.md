# Book IX: scansion check and delivery tags

I read all 484 rows against `latin/clean/09.txt`, looking hardest at the 14 `amb`, 16 `var` and 4 `frag` rows. **43 verses** are corrected in `scan/manual/09.tsv`. `python3 tools/check_manual.py 09` prints `09 OK`.

## Corrections, by kind

**1. Wrong quantities: the foot pattern changes (23 verses).**
- **24**: `Ō flōs | Ītălĭ | ē, iŭvĕ…` (SDDSDX). The scanner had ictus on short *ta* and a spondee *iuve*.
- **53**: `Fōrtū | nāt' īn | quīt iŭvĕ…` (SSDSDX). The scanner had a dactyl *Fortuna-* and a spondee *iuve*.
- **40, 120**: *famae* is long-long (`fa|mé tibi`, `Iám fa|mé quod`). The scanner had a dactyl *fame* and a spondee *tibi*.
- **75**: *nōbīs* (`no|bís — ani|múm`). The scanner had *nobis* inside a dactyl. The dash moves to the penthemimeral.
- **81, 444, 462**: `Pīĕrĭ-` keeps 4 syllables. The scanner's synizesis *Pye-* put ictus on short *-ri-*, *-se* and *-dum*.
- **116**: `Īncŏlă | Cīrrhae|ō — tŏtĭ…` (DSDSDX). The scanner had *Incola* as a spondee.
- **174**: *vōtīs* (`di|ú — vo|tís ani|móque`, DDSDDX).
- **195**: *mĭnĭmē* (`vi|déns — mini|mé?`).
- **196**: *meae* elided before *Aegaeō* (`Quánta me'! |Ége|ó — dif…`, DSSSDX).
- **210**: *dēpŏsĭta'st* (DDSDDX). The scanner made *-po-* long.
- **254**: *suās* has 2 syllables (`Prímiti|ásque su|ás — sanc…`, DDSSDX). The scanner read *suas* as one syllable.
- **259**: *extrēmā* (`éxtre|má — rele|géns ab o|rígine`).
- **274**: *Cētĕra. Prōcēdō* (DSSDDX).
- **293**: `Phoebŭs Ă|tlāntē|ō rĕcrĕ|ābāt|gūrgĭtĕ` (DSDSDX). The scanner had a spondaic *gurgĭtĕ* and a short *-ā-* in *recreābat*.
- **310**: *ŭbĭ* is short (`redi|éns — ubi |prím'e|mérsit E|óo`, DDDSDX).
- **327**: *Phoebēōs* has 3 syllables (`Fébe|ós fre|nábat — a|géns`, SSDSDX).
- **409**: *hērōum* has 3 syllables (`Héro|úm vete|rúm`). The scanner had a spondee *vete*.
- **410**: *Irrĭta nē Grāī* (`Írrita |né Gra|í — fie|rént`, DSDSDX).
- **430**: *Parthĕnŏpēă* (DDDDDX).
- **456**: *Lēthaeus* (`ét Le|téus in |ánnos`, DSDSDX). The scanner had a spondee *-us in*.

**2. Wrong u/v decisions (7 verses).**
- **27**: *Voluebam* is from *volvo*, so it becomes **Vólve|bám** (vowel *u* became consonant *v*).
- **128**: *Transiuit* becomes **Tránsi|vít** (*trānsīvit*).
- **133**: *siluere* is from *sileo* (*sĭlŭēre*), so it becomes **silu|ére** (consonant *v* became vowel *u*).
- **302**: *siluit* becomes **silu|ít** (same verb, *sileo*).
- **178**: *voluique* is from *volo*: Ennius *wanted* to touch Homer's feet, and the translation agrees. It becomes **volu|íque**.
- **250**: *vidue* = *viduae*, which has 3 syllables (**vidu|é**).
- **142**: *Precipue* had a consonant *v* (`Precipv'`). It becomes **Précipu'|íllus|trés — …** (DSDSDX).

**3. Wrong elisions (3 verses).**
- **52**: *saxum Eacide*. Hiatus cannot fit the line, so the elision is required: **sax'|Éaci|dé — titu…**
- **391**: *templa Iovis*. There is no elision before a consonantal *I*, so it becomes **témpla Io|vís**.
- **432**: *tellure iacentem*. Same rule, so it becomes **tellúre ia|céntem**.

**4. Caesura dash only (9 verses).**
- **70, 125, 180, 466**: removed. The dash sat right after *et* or *ex*, or between *brevitate* and *tua*, and the semicolon or comma nearby already gives the pause.
- **163, 278, 394**: moved from after a preposition (*in*, *inter*, *ad*) to the hephthemimeral: *dubió —*, *fontés —*, *reliquós —*.
- **363**: moved from after an elided *et* to the trithemimeral: *Indignós — patri'aspect'et…*
- **451**: moved from the trithemimeral to the penthemimeral after the prodelided *sorte'st*: *Utendum sorte'st — et sidera…*
- **120, 210** also lose their dash, because after the correction a comma or full stop stands at the real caesura.

**5. Line 448 (`frag`).** *Nosque utinam - - -* is a deliberate break-off, and the words still make a complete hexameter (DDSSDX). I added the `|` marks: `Témpora! |Nósqu'uti|nám … Ne|quícquam |vána pre|cámur!`

## Tags (`tags/09.tsv`)
There are 49 tags: 25 at section starts and 24 inside sections, about one inner tag per 20 verses. They follow the mood of each passage:
- **Homeward voyage and the talk with Ennius**: *serene, stately* → *calm narration* → Scipio asking Ennius to speak *(tender, pleading)* → Ennius on fame *(reverent, solemn; wistful at 45)*.
- **On poets and poetry**: the lament that Scipio lacks a fitting poet *(indignant, wistful)* → *warm, earnest* → *measured, thoughtful*, with *stern* at the attack on liars (103) → the laurel *(solemn, reverent)*.
- **The dream**: *hushed* at 132, where the sailors fall silent → *awed, adoring* for Homer → *hushed, tense* on the eve of Zama → *awed* when Homer appears → *tender, solemn* for his greeting → *anguished* for the question about his blindness → *grave, serene* for his answer.
- **The prophecy of Petrarch**: *solemn, prophetic* → *proud, prophetic*, with *tender* for the simile of the late-born son → *stately, measured* → *eager*.
- **Waking and the voyage on**: *startled, urgent* for the trumpet → *tranquil* night voyage → *bright, joyful* for Sicily.
- **The triumph**:
  - the invocation is *reverent*, then *jubilant* for the triumph morning;
  - Scipio's entry is *majestic, stately*;
  - the captives are *grave*, and Syphax is *sorrowful, pitying*;
  - the spoils are *proud, vigorous*, and the freed Romans *joyful*;
  - the army is *triumphant, majestic*, with *thunderous* for the trumpets;
  - the laurel is *proud, solemn*, with *quiet pride* for Petrarch's own crowning.
- **Closing sections**:
  - the refusal to write Scipio's end is *sorrowful, grave*;
  - the lament for King Robert is *grieving, tender*, then *anxious, foreboding*;
  - the late age is *wistful, bitter*, turning *hopeful*;
  - the charge to the book is *tender, earnest*, then *fervent*, and the defiance of Time at the close is *defiant, majestic*.
